What can you do in an online squishy game?

An online squishy game gives you a virtual toy to press and reshape on a screen. In My Squishy Lab, the experience starts before the first squeeze, with a workshop where you choose the shape, colour, feel and fillings of your creation.

That making process gives each toy a little history. A pink dumpling filled with fruit slices feels like a different project from a blue star dotted with gems, even when both begin at the same workbench.

Play a squishy game without downloading it

The free browser edition opens from the Play free button on this website. You can start on a computer, phone or tablet without creating a player account.

On a phone or tablet, rotate the screen to landscape so the tools fit around the making area. The website and its guides work in portrait too, so you can read an idea before turning your device sideways to try it.

Your browser needs an internet connection to load the game files. If you want a shortcut for another visit, the Download page explains how to add the browser game to your home screen.

Making is part of the game

Begin with a mould, then work through colour mixing, material selection and inclusions. Each stage gives you a specific decision, which makes it easier to experiment without planning the whole toy in advance.

For a first attempt, keep one choice simple. Choose a single colour and use the filling as your main decoration, or leave the filling plain and concentrate on comparing how the different feels recover after a squeeze.

Pour when the recipe is ready and let the mould fill and settle. Reveal becomes available after that step, taking you from a mixture in the workshop to a toy you can play with.

Poking, squeezing and stretching

The finished squishy supports different kinds of interaction. A poke presses a small area, while the hand squeeze changes a wider part of the shape.

On a touch screen, use two fingers to try stretching. Release the toy between attempts so you can watch it recover and see how its chosen material responds.

The Play and View controls serve different purposes. Play is where you interact with the toy, while View lets you look at it from other angles and inspect details you might miss from the front.

What gives you a reason to return?

Saving a creation adds it to your local collection, so the next visit can begin with a favourite you already made. Your shelf becomes a record of the shapes, colours and recipes you have tried.

The Daily Workshop offers a reward and a rotating recipe challenge. You can also make gifts for fictional workshop friends and use available decorations to personalise your setting.

Try giving yourself a small creative brief for a session. Make a fruit shop collection, create three toys in the same colour family, or compare one shape in each of the three materials.

How does saving work?

Progress is stored in the browser on the device you use. A toy saved on your tablet will need a backup transfer if you want to continue with it in another browser or on another device.

The Grown-ups area includes export and import tools. Keep a backup before clearing browser data, since removing website storage can also remove the local collection.

Choosing your first project

A useful starting project has one clear idea, such as a favourite colour or a particular filling. That gives you something to recognise when you reveal the finished toy, while leaving room to explore the controls.

Once you have saved it, make a second version with just one change. Seeing the pair together helps you decide whether you prefer a different material, a quieter colour or a fuller mixture.

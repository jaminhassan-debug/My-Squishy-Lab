Squishy Filling Ideas: Fruit, Gems, Pearls & Sparkle

My Squishy Lab · 19 November 2026

Choose squishy fillings with a clear theme. Explore fruit slices, gems, pearls, sprinkles and foil shapes, plus tips for amounts and unlocking hearts.

What are squishy fillings in the game?

Squishy fillings are the little pieces you add to a virtual toy's mixture before pouring. In My Squishy Lab, they are also called inclusions, and they become part of the recipe for your finished creation.

The filling scene organises the choices into Sparkle, Beads and Charms. These categories help you find a look, whether you want a fruit themed dumpling, a gem filled star or a simple toy with just a few decorative pieces.

Choose one main ingredient first

Begin with the inclusion you most want to see when you reveal the squishy. Pick its type and amount, then decide whether the design needs another ingredient.

A single filling family makes the idea easier to recognise. It also gives you a useful first attempt to compare with a fuller version, especially if you keep the same mould and body colour.

Fruit slices for a colourful recipe

Fruit slices include strawberry, kiwi and orange choices. Select the individual fruit you want when the fruit slice picker opens, then continue with the amount for that batch.

Try making a fruit collection where every toy uses the same mould and a different slice. You can match the body to the fruit or choose a contrasting colour to make the inclusion easier to notice.

For a mixed fruit idea, add another fruit in a later batch. Start with two types and inspect the finished result before planning a toy with every fruit option.

Gems for a little treasure theme

The gem family offers round and diamond forms. Compare the two shapes in separate creations if you want to see which suits your chosen mould.

A single body colour can make a gem themed toy easier to read. Try a quiet background for the gem shapes first, then compare it with a more colourful mixture on your next attempt.

Gems also work as the shared ingredient across a collection. A star, a dumpling and a blob can feel connected when the same gem type appears inside all three.

Pearls for a simple repeated detail

Pearls give you one repeated rounded shape to work with. They are useful when the body colour or the mould is already the main feature of your design.

Try making two matching toys with different pearl amounts. That comparison can help you decide how much empty space you like around the inclusions.

Keep the other recipe choices consistent while comparing. Changing the colour, size and material at the same time makes it harder to work out which version of the filling you prefer.

Sprinkles for a playful mixture

Sprinkles are another option in the Beads category. Try them when you want a small repeated detail rather than a large charm shape.

A bakery themed dumpling is one idea to explore, especially if you plan to display it in Bao Bakery later. Choose the body colour first, then use the sprinkle amount to decide how busy the finished recipe should feel.

Foil stars and hearts for sparkle

Foil stars sit in the Sparkle category and provide a recognisable shape for a themed toy. They can be the main ingredient in a night sky idea or a recurring detail across several colours.

Foil hearts unlock after you have tried all three material feels. Free Play also makes them available, so the Grown-ups preference is another way to explore those shapes.

If a heart choice is locked, compare Soft Foam, Dense Gel and Bouncy Jelly as you play. The material samples let you explore their differences while working towards that discovery.

How much filling should you add?

The amount choices add batches of 8, 16 or 24 pieces. A recipe supports up to six batches, with a total limit of 120 pieces.

Start with a smaller amount when you want to see the body colour clearly. Add more in another attempt when your goal is a fuller mixture with lots of repeated detail.

The Undo control removes the most recently added batch. Use it before continuing if you want to rethink a choice or make room for another ingredient.

Inspect the contents after reveal

Pour the prepared mixture, let it settle and continue to Reveal when that control becomes available. Then use View to look at the toy from different angles.

Save a version you like before beginning another recipe. A small series of toys with one ingredient changed is a practical way to discover your favourite balance of colour, filling and feel.

Squishy Colour Combinations: 7 Ideas to Try

My Squishy Lab · 19 November 2026

Explore seven squishy colour ideas, from pink and blue mixes to fruit and gemstone themes, with practical tips for a coordinated toy collection.

Start with a colour idea you can recognise

A squishy colour combination works well when you know what you want the finished toy to remind you of. That might be a favourite fruit, a night sky or a set of toys that belong on the same shelf.

In My Squishy Lab, you choose colours during the making flow and stir the mixture before moving on. The colour you reveal is a digital blend, so treat each recipe as something to explore in the game.

Pink and blue for a violet direction

Hot pink and electric blue make a useful starting pair when you want to explore violet tones. Mix them and watch the result before deciding whether the next toy needs a different balance.

Use one filling family in the finished design, such as gems or foil stars. A single inclusion type gives the blended body a clear visual theme and makes it easier to remember what you changed.

Blue and sunshine for a fresh green

Electric blue with sunshine takes the mixture in a green direction. Try the pair when your idea is a garden themed toy or a collection inspired by fruit colours.

Kiwi slices are one filling to explore alongside this idea. Check how clearly they appear against the body, then make a second version with a simpler colour if you want more contrast.

Pink and sunshine for a warm combination

Hot pink and sunshine make a warm mix that is useful for orange and coral inspired projects. It is a different starting point from a blue based recipe, so it can add variety to a shelf of cooler toys.

Try pairing the idea with orange slices or sprinkles. The two filling choices create different visual stories even when the body begins with the same colour combination.

Blue and emerald for a lagoon theme

Electric blue and emerald give you a route towards blue green shades. A star, cloud or rounded blob can carry that colour theme, depending on which mould you want to try.

Pearls are a simple inclusion to explore with it. Keep the first version restrained, then use another saved toy to compare a larger amount of the same ingredient.

Violet and pink for a berry theme

Violet and hot pink give you another way to explore the pink to purple part of the palette. Compare this blend with your pink and blue experiment so you can decide which direction you prefer.

Strawberry slices or gems can provide the main detail inside the toy. You can also keep a second version with fewer inclusions to see more of the mixed body colour.

One colour with contrasting fillings

A colour idea can begin with a single body colour. Choose electric blue, sunshine or hot pink on its own, then let a different coloured inclusion provide the contrast.

This approach is useful when a mixed colour looks busier than you intended. It also makes a good reference toy, because you can compare later blends against the original colour.

A coordinated set across several squishies

Your colour combination can run across a collection as well as inside one mixture. Make several toys that share one ingredient, such as foil stars, and give each a different body colour.

Keep the mould or material consistent for a set that is easy to compare. Alternatively, repeat the colour and change the mould to make a family of shapes for your shelf.

Change one choice between attempts

When a recipe gives you an unexpected result, start the next toy by changing one thing. Choose a different colour pair, simplify the inclusions or keep the body colour and compare two materials.

Adding every available colour is unlikely to show you which choice made the difference. A small sequence of experiments gives you more useful information and a collection you can revisit.

Use the room as inspiration

A selected room can give you a starting palette for your next squishy. Think of greens for Mint Greenhouse, cooler colours for Stargazing Loft or warmer ideas for Bao Bakery.

The toy does not need to match every object in the room. One repeated colour or filling theme is enough to connect your creation with its display setting.

Save the versions you want to compare

Finish and save your favourite experiments so you can inspect them together later. Your local shelf is a better reference than trying to remember exactly how an earlier mix looked.

Before moving to another device, export a backup from Grown-ups. That keeps your saved collection available to transfer when you are ready to try the next colour idea elsewhere.
